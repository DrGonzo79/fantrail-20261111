# Office Hours — FanTrail

## Diagnostic

- **Specific customer:** the producer at a 10–100-person experiential agency responsible for a brand activation and the post-event recap. The fan is the user, not the economic buyer.
- **Painful status quo:** the producer pays for an offsite, distributes through fragmented fan channels, then defends performance with weak impression estimates and disconnected QR scans.
- **Desperate use case:** 30 days before a tentpole, prove incremental visits across one activation or a coordinated quest without forcing attendees to install an event app.
- **Narrowest paid wedge:** a manually operated, co-branded pin plus signed QR check-in and a one-page aggregate recap for one activation at $750. Do not start with a citywide platform.
- **Observation plan:** shadow one producer building a recap; inventory every source, consent step, dedupe rule and metric. Conduct five fan intercepts to learn whether the free map changes plans.
- **Validation plan:** sell 10 agency prospects a one-event pilot. Success is two deposits and one buyer agreeing in writing on the metric definition before any live map build. Kill if no deposit after 20 qualified conversations.
- **Future fit:** only after repeated pilots should the system add cross-event benchmarks, agency workspaces and sponsor-funded quests. The defensible asset is trusted event-level measurement and channel partnerships—not map code.

## Decision

The report supports real fan frustration and adjacent spend, but brand willingness to pay this specific operator is unproven. Build only the demo and a concierge sales test; avoid inventory ingestion, attendee tracking or a live event promise before deposits.
