# CEO review

## Challenge

The first draft overvalued the map. Fans already have free maps and an unofficial blog; software is not the scarce asset. It also treated the $400K–$500K ARR ceiling too literally despite seasonal events and quest-heavy project revenue. The ICP was too broad, the check-in metric risked sounding like causal attribution, and a six-stop demo could imply an operational readiness the founder has not earned.

GTM is the bottleneck: search volume is negligible, incumbent communities control attention, and brands buy through agencies of record. Five $750 pins barely pay for event-week operations; a $25K quest may require procurement, creative, prizes and insurance. Cross-event benchmarks could become a moat, but only after trusted distribution and metric definitions. Architecture must handle stale inventory, token abuse, consent, event spikes and degraded list mode—not just draw pins.

## Decisions applied

1. Reframed FanTrail as **measurement with a free discovery lead magnet**, not another map.
2. Narrowed the paying ICP to experiential agency producers and the first paid unit to one $750 activation pilot.
3. Removed live location and causal footfall claims; check-ins are explicit simulated codes and production metrics must be aggregate and pre-defined.
4. Made all brands, locations, geometry and results visibly fictional.
5. Added invalid-input, unknown-stop, rejected-code, mobile-overflow and backend integration tests.
6. Set a falsifiable gate: two deposits from 20 qualified agency conversations and one fan-media partner before production infrastructure.
7. Kept FastAPI undeployed and documented observability/failure modes rather than pretending GitHub Pages is full stack.

**Verdict:** ship the prototype as a sales/learning artifact. Do not build a live convention map until paid demand and distribution access exist.
