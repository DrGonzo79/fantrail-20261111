# Source research — 2026-11-11

- **Idea:** Software for brands to put their convention pop-ups on one map and see who shows up
- **Public source:** https://www.ideabrowser.com/hub/ideas/today
- **IB Index / category:** 6.3 / B2B
- **Reported economics:** $400K–$500K ARR ceiling; $30K–$80K year one; 90–120 days to first revenue; $3K–$15K capital; moderate difficulty.
- **Buyer:** experiential agencies, brand experiential leads, convention organizers and fan media around SDCC, Anime Expo, SXSW, TIFF, ComplexCon and similar tentpoles.
- **Painful status quo:** fans refresh Reddit and an unofficial blog, miss registrations, and navigate fragmented offsite information. Brands spend heavily but receive recap decks led by estimated impressions rather than attributable visits.
- **Wedge/gap:** a browser-based independent city layer for offsites, merch drops, creator events and parties. Free attendee discovery earns distribution; aggregate QR check-ins and a sponsor quest are the paid measurement product.
- **Why now:** the report cites city passports and brand scavenger hunts shipping in 2026, plus record 2024 experiential spend. It says EventTrack 2026 found 84% of consumer and 86% of B2B marketers planned budget increases, while lead/data capture overtook foot traffic as a priority. These are report-derived claims, not independently audited here.
- **Demand signals:** direct Reddit complaints about sold-out signups, missing activations, and weak early information; SDCC reportedly draws 130K–150K+ attendees. Community emotion establishes fan pain, not brand purchase intent.
- **Keyword/distribution reality:** reported search volume is tiny—`sdcc offsite` 30/month and `comic con offsite events` 40/month. The first audience must come from r/SDCC (reported 22.1K followers), r/comiccon (72.2K), and SDCC Unofficial Blog, not SEO or paid ads.
- **Competitors:** SDCC Unofficial Blog owns offsite discovery; sdccmap.us reportedly had 200+ pins; Goosechase sells scavenger hunts; ATIV ScanHunt lists $19.99–$2,499.99 monthly tiers; Map D sells event mapping; PheedLoop offers event software; NVE, Kap Media and FG|PG are agency channels or substitutes.
- **Offer ladder:** free map; $750 promoted activation pin with reservation link/check-in count/recap; $25K sponsor-funded 8–12-stop quest; 5–10% affiliate commission on eligible reservations.
- **Napkin model:** 150 agency prospects, 60 outbound contacts, assumed 20% replies, 12 meetings, assumed 40% close, five $750 pins = $3,750; one $25K quest makes $28,750. The response and close rates are assumptions. Report pricing anchors include Map D at $1,500–$2,750/event and Goosechase upgrades at $99–$649.
- **Biggest risks:** free maps own fan distribution; the unofficial blog can add monetization; agency-of-record procurement slows sales; demand is seasonal; a missed event destroys a quarter; check-in fraud/privacy can make measurement untrustworthy.
- **Founder fit:** event-production and agency-sales experience, named convention relationships, local operational presence, and comfort with seasonal revenue matter more than software skill. Product-only founders are a poor fit.
- **Execution demands (report):** sales 10/10, industry access 9/10, community 8/10, build 4/10, capital 3/10.
- **Cited domains/sources in the report:** EventTrack/Snapbar, PQ Media, Destination Toronto, NYC Tourism/West Side Spirit, AOL coverage of Lancôme, Event Marketer, BizBash, Reddit, SDCC Unofficial Blog, Goosechase, ATIV, Map D, PheedLoop, 360X Events and Promotions.

## Prototype choice

FanTrail tests the narrow operational loop: select activations → generate a feasible trail → submit a simulated stop code. It omits live maps, reservations, personal location, sponsor billing, attribution claims, and production analytics until demand and consent mechanics are validated.
